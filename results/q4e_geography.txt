Two dense high-value clusters are visible: one around the San Francisco Bay Area (~ -122.4 lon, ~37.7-37.9 lat) and one around the Los Angeles / coastal Southern California area (~ -118.2 to -117.9 lon, ~33.7-34.1 lat).

Neighboring census blocks share unobserved geographic covariates (school quality, distance to the coast, local zoning, employer proximity, crime, amenities) that are not in the eight predictors. This means nearby rows are correlated rather than independent draws, violating the i.i.d. assumption. Consequence for a random train/test split: because nearby blocks end up on both sides of the split, performance metrics are optimistic (a form of leakage) -- the model can partly 'memorize' the local neighborhood pattern from a training block and exploit it on a geographically adjacent test block, so a random split overstates how well the model will generalize to a genuinely new, geographically distinct area. A spatial (e.g. block-group or region-based) split would give a more honest estimate.
